Crafting is EmberQuest's economic spine: every weapon, armor piece, tool, and boat of the game's item pool comes out of it, and the system runs on a **nine-tier ladder** that stretches from day-one wooden gear to the current endgame. This overview covers how the pieces fit together before you commit materials to anything expensive.

## What materials does crafting use?

The base resources are split across the gathering skills: **logs** (Chopping), **bars** (Mining), **fish** (Fishing and Spearfishing), **gems and resin** (Collecting), plus drops from combat and world content. Each item category draws from a different mix — wood armor eats logs, metal armor eats bars, weapons blend metal and wood, and food crafting (Cooking) sits beside the main crafting tree.

## How do the tiers work?

Each tier consumes meaningfully more (and rarer) materials than the last, and the items each tier unlocks stay relevant longer as you go. The official wiki lists **crafting tiers 1 through 9** as separate pages, which tells you the intended shape: you are not meant to hit tier 9 quickly, and written guides that promise otherwise are guessing. Practical pacing for a new player: live in tiers 1–3 for your first weeks, treat 4–6 as your first real project, and see 7+ as milestone goals.

## What is enchanting and what are glyphs?

**Enchanting** upgrades existing equipment — the wiki pairs it with glyphs as its mechanism, and glyph details were still marked "soon" when we last checked (October 11, 2026). The safe planning assumption: enchanting is where gear that would otherwise be outgrown gets extended, and gems/resin are the resources it consumes. If you gather gems early, you are prepaying for enchantments later.

## When should you craft versus buy?

The marketplace is the pressure valve for grindy materials: logging an hour of Chopping and selling to buy mid-tier components is often faster than gathering them directly. For your **first gear set, craft it yourself** (you cannot afford marketplace prices in your first hours), and for later sets, price both options — the material-to-gold conversion changes with what the whole server is training that week.

## What should you craft first?

1. **Tools** — fishing rod, chopping axe, pickaxe. These gate every gathering skill, and they are cheap.
2. **A full tier 1–2 set for your class** — e.g. wood armor for light classes, metal for the [Warrior](/classes/warrior/) grind.
3. **One stack of consumables** — food or potions from Cooking/enchanting-adjacent lines, so you stop paying the marketplace premium at every boss.
4. **Then, a weapon** — because weapons are the most expensive per item and the most affected by which [class](/guides/which-class-to-pick/) you settled on.

## Where do skills come in?

Crafting itself is a trainable skill, and its level gates what you can make far more than any item does. That is the reason our [skills overview](/skills/skills-overview/) treats Crafting as the priority production skill, and why the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) tells you to bank your first resources instead of selling them: your own crafting progression is the cheapest gear in the game.
