EmberQuest has **10 skills**, and they are the game's real progression system — classes pick your combat baseline, but skills decide what you can make, sell, and eat. Here is every skill, what it actually does, and which ones pay first.

## What are the 10 skills?

Gathering: **Chopping, Mining, Fishing, Spearfishing, Collecting**. Production: **Cooking, Crafting, Enchanting**. Combat: **Archery, Melee**. That is the whole list — three production skills turn five gathering skills into everything the economy runs on.

## What do the gathering skills feed?

- **Chopping** — wood. Feeds the wood armor line, tools, and the early tiers of crafting. The most beginner-friendly money skill in the game.
- **Mining** — ore into bars: the metal economy. Everything metal-armor and weapon-related starts here.
- **Fishing** — fish, used for cooking and sold reliably. Low-risk, gentle, and one of the canonical cozy money loops.
- **Spearfishing** — the higher-intensity coastal cousin of fishing, for catch that regular rods cannot reach.
- **Collecting** — gathering rare materials and collectibles in the world. Slower and luck-flavored; it pairs with exploration rather than replacing it.

## What do the production skills unlock?

- **Crafting** — the game's backbone. Crafting runs on **tiers 1 through 9**, each tier consuming far more materials than the last: tier 1 is your starter set, and by the top tiers you are feeding it from multiple gathering skills at once (the full ladder is broken down in our [crafting overview](/crafting/crafting-overview/)).
- **Cooking** — fish and foraged ingredients into consumables. In a game where heal bonus is a class stat, food is a combat resource, not flavor.
- **Enchanting** — upgrades equipment using resources like gems and resin, through the **glyph** system (glyph details were still marked "soon" on the official wiki as of October 2026).

## Which combat skill should you train?

**Archery** is ranged; **Melee** is close. Your class suggests the natural pair ([Archer](/classes/archer/) and [Ranger](/classes/ranger/) → Archery; [Warrior](/classes/warrior/) and [Assassin](/classes/assassin/) → Melee), but nothing stops you from cross-training, and everyone needs some combat ability for bosses, world events, and the occasional PvP zone.

## Do skills have levels and caps?

Yes — skills train through use, and the official wiki shows per-level value curves for classes and items, meaning the game tracks skill levels numerically. The current level cap is not published as a single number; the practical answer is that every skill can be pushed high enough to matter, and the fastest skill progress comes from gameplay in that skill's activity, not from a menu.

## What is the best skill order for a new player?

For your first week: **one gathering skill you enjoy** (Chopping or Mining for crafters, Fishing for pacifists), **Crafting** until your first full gear set, **one combat skill** to survive field content, then branch. The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) walks through exactly this, and [how to play with friends](/guides/how-to-play-with-friends/) covers why gathering next to guildmates is the best QoL feature in the game.

## Why do skill levels matter so much?

Because skill progress is **shared across classes and permanent** — it survives class choices, gear resets, and patches. Long-term EmberQuest players are their skills, not their class. That is why this site treats the [10-skill system](#what-are-the-10-skills) as the game's spine and the class guides as the entry point.
