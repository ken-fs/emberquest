EmberQuest is an MMO first, so "can I play with friends" is really "how well does co-op work". The short answer: yes — parties, guilds, group PvP events, and world bosses are all in the feature list. Here is what is confirmed, what to expect, and what nobody has published yet.

## How do parties work?

The game supports **forming a party** for shared questing and combat. Party play is how most players approach the world boss and group PvP events, and questing together is supported directly — one of EmberQuest's stated pillars is completing quests together rather than building a fully solo experience. The exact party size cap is not published; treat 4 as the working assumption for group content until the game says otherwise.

## How do guilds work?

**Joining a guild** is supported and is the game's social anchor: guild chat, group events, and the plain logistics of "who is online to fight the boss" live there. There is no published guild leveling or territory system at launch, so for now a guild is a friend list plus coordination — which is genuinely most of what you need in a cozy MMO.

## What group content is there to do?

- **World bosses** — the classic "everyone nearby piles in" fight. Bring your main combat skill trained.
- **Group PvP events** — scheduled, opt-in fights rather than open griefing; the PvP zone details are still marked "soon" on the official wiki.
- **Arenas** — structured PvP matches.
- **Shared skilling** — nothing stops you from fishing next to three guildmates for an evening, and in a game with 10 skills, company is the best QoL feature ever shipped.

## Can you play with friends across browser and Steam?

Both clients play the same game, so cross-play works in the sense that everyone is in the same world. What is not yet documented is whether your **account and progress** move freely between the browser build and the Steam build — ask in the game's Discord before committing hundreds of hours to one client if that matters to you.

## What about PvP — is it forced?

No. PvP is opt-in through arenas, group events, and specific zones; the world itself is not a free-for-all, and the game is explicitly pitched as cozy-first. If you never want to fight another player, the combat skills you train for PvE (see the [skills overview](/skills/skills-overview/)) are enough for everything non-PvP.

## What should a group do first?

One evening plan that works: everyone finishes the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) solo, picks classes that complement each other (one [Warrior](/classes/warrior/) as anchor, ranged damage behind — see [which class to pick](/guides/which-class-to-pick/)), spends an hour gathering, then meets at the first world boss. That thin loop — gather, craft, fight, repeat — is the entire game at every scale, and it is much better with friends.

## Is there a fresh server for new players?

Yes. On October 18, 2026 the developer launched a new server in the US region (Chicago). It gives you a fresh start, so you don't have to catch up with players who've been around for months.

The announcement says it's open to accounts created before October 10th. Other regions and transfers aren't mentioned. If you want to play together, make sure everyone picks the same server.
