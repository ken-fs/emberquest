EmberQuest releases on Steam on **October 15, 2026** and has been playable in the browser at emberquest.io for months. This guide covers the first hour in either client: what the tutorial touches, which early decisions stick, and what you can safely ignore until later.

## Should you play in the browser or on Steam?

Play in whichever client is convenient — the browser build at emberquest.io and the Steam release are the same game. The Steam version adds the usual platform conveniences (friends list, overlay, updates through the client), while the browser version needs nothing but a landscape screen. Whether progress carries between the two has not been confirmed yet; if that matters to you, check the game's Discord before investing a lot of time in one client.

## What does the tutorial actually teach?

The tutorial is short and hands-on: you talk to **Tutorial Ted**, learn movement and combat basics, and get pointed at your first tools and gathering spots. Treat it as a control tutorial rather than a progression guide — nothing in it locks you into a class or a skill path, and combat mechanics (dash, stamina, hooks) are worth practicing right away because they carry into every later system.

## Which class should you pick?

Four classes exist: **Warrior, Archer, Assassin, and Ranger**. The short version: Warrior is the tankiest and best with crowd control, Archer is pure ranged damage, Assassin trades health for burst and the game's only stealth access, Ranger sits in the middle with sustain. If you want the full breakdown — HP, armor, crit profiles and what each class is actually good at — read [which class to pick](/classes/) before you commit.

## Which skills should you train first?

There are **10 skills**: Chopping, Cooking, Mining, Fishing, Spearfishing, Collecting, Crafting, Enchanting, Archery, and Melee. In your first session the practical order is:

1. **Whatever your toolkit starts with** — gather the resources the tutorial quests ask for.
2. **Crafting** — turns those resources into your first real gear.
3. **Cooking and Fishing** — cheap, safe income and consumables you will use constantly.
4. **Combat skills (Archery or Melee)** — enough to hold your own near town; you can specialize later.

There is no skill respec mentioned anywhere in the game, but skills level through use rather than one-time picks, so "mistakes" mostly cost time, not builds.

## What should you do in the first hour that pays off later?

- **Finish the tutorial quest line** — it hands you the tools that make every skill trainable.
- **Bank your first resources instead of selling them** — early crafting tiers eat a lot of logs and bars, and buying them back from the marketplace is more expensive than most new players expect (see our [crafting overview](/crafting/crafting-overview/) for what tiers demand).
- **Poke every system once** — open the marketplace, take one PvP-adjacent quest if you are brave, and try the party system: see [how to play with friends](/guides/how-to-play-with-friends/). You never know which loop will be your favorite.
- **Don't chase rare gear yet** — the economy is young and prices swing hard at launch; your first crafted set will carry you fine.

## What is there to do after the first hour?

EmberQuest is a sandbox: skilling, questing, bosses, world PvP, arenas, group events, and a player marketplace all run in parallel. Most players settle into one money skill, one combat style, and whatever social loop their guild does. If you want a structured next step, our [skills overview](/skills/skills-overview/) explains what each of the 10 skills unlocks down the line.
